## 3. Cash profit vs economic profit

Implemented in `economics.py`:

```
CASH PROFIT      = realized resale − every real cash outflow
                   (bid, premium, tax, vehicle, fuel, dump, fees, returns,
                    packing, supplies, sales tax remitted, expected deposit loss)
ECONOMIC PROFIT  = cash profit − labor hours × value of time
cash ROI         = cash profit / cash invested
cash profit/hour = cash profit / labor hours
```

What changed from v1, and why:

| v1 | v2 | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Labor at $20/h subtracted inside "net profit"; min-profit and ROI rules used it | Labor never subtracted from cash. Rules: cash profit ≥ $150, cash ROI ≥ 50%, **cash profit/hour ≥ $25** (unchanged), low-case cash ≥ −$75, cash needed ≤ bankroll | A labor guess shouldn't decide affordability. Time value stays an explicit, adjustable hurdle |
| 5% "risk reserve" subtracted as a cost | Removed (0). Added 2% misc cash costs (supplies, meetup travel, breakage) | The reserve wasn't a payment and double-counted the valuation haircut and the low case |
| 7% blended selling fee | 75% local at 0% + 25% eBay (13.6% + $0.40) = 3.4% | Sourced fee schedules |
| Tax 8.25% at purchase, none on sales | **Licensed reseller:** purchase exempt; **8.2% remitted on local sales** (absorbed in price). Toggle `tax_regime: informal` | Colorado requires a regular reseller to be licensed and collect tax. eBay collects its own |
| Economic profit reported at one rate | Reported at **$0 / $15 / $25 / $40 per hour** everywhere | Your request |

Not in the per-locker model, but real:

- **Self-employment tax** ≈ 14.1% of annual net profit once it exceeds $400
  (IRS Schedule SE).
- Federal/state income tax on top.

Treat a year's cash profit as roughly **80–85% after tax** for a low-income
student. Confirm with a tax preparer.

Labor at $1,000 resale and $50 average sale: 17–23 hours. **About 80% of it
is selling** (15 min per listing, 25 min per sale).

What the grids say:

1. **The cash downside of a small locker is small and bounded.** The worst
   cell at a $150 bid is −$381 (5x10, HIGH disposal, only $250 of resale).
2. **The answer depends on the value of your time.** At $0/h almost anything
   above ~$500 of resale works. At $25/h a 5x5 needs ~$530–950 and a 5x10
   ~$690–1,490 at $50 average sale.
3. **Average sale price dominates economic profit.** Each sale costs ~44
   minutes. A $25 sale nets ~$21 cash, so at $40/h every additional sale
   *loses* money. This is the real "value density" effect: dollars **per item
   sold** matter even more than dollars per cubic foot.
4. **5x5 beats 5x10 at the same resale.** Break-evens are ~15–40% lower,
   because of fewer trips and less dump weight. But a 5x10 probably holds more
   resale. The grid can't say which is better. Only the resale distribution by
   size can, and only real units reveal that. **The small-unit hypothesis is
   untested, not supported.**
5. **Disposal matters more for 5x10 than 5x5.** HIGH vs LOW moves 5x10 cash by
   ~$363 at the same resale. On a 5x5 it's ~$200.

## 7. Proposed under-30-second capture workflow

Goal: you never type fields. Every step is something you already do while
looking at an auction.

**Per auction (target ≤ 30 s):**

1. In the auction app or site, take **1 screenshot of the listing header**
   (unit size, current bid, end time, facility, fees). Take **1 screenshot of
   the photo gallery or best photo**.
2. Tap Share → Copy link, and paste it into a "LockerLab" note (or share the
   screenshot with the link into one album).
3. Optional one-tap tags in the note: `L`/`M`/`H` disposal look, and
   `tools`, `outdoor`, `electronics`, `furniture`.

**Once a day at the laptop (2–4 min for 10 auctions):**

4. Drop the screenshots and the note into `lockerlab/inbox/` (AirDrop, Drive
   sync or USB).
5. `lockerlab ingest inbox/` (**to build**): a vision model reads *your
   screenshots*, extracts size, bid, end time, facility, city and fees with a
   confidence per field, and pairs screenshots with links. The screenshots are
   stored as raw evidence.
6. `lockerlab review` (**to build**): one screen per auction shows the extracted
   fields next to the screenshot. Enter to accept, or type a correction.
   Low-confidence fields are highlighted. Rows become observations exactly like
   the CSV path (provenance: `capture_method=screenshot`,
   `parser_version=vision_vN`).

**Outcomes:** the day after an auction ends, open its link and take one
screenshot (sold price, or cancelled). Same inbox. About 10 s each.

Expected time: ~15–20 s capture + ~10–15 s review ≈ **25–35 s per auction**.
Model cost: a few cents per auction. Price it before building, and cache by
image hash.

**Before building 5–6 on StorageTreasures/Lockerfox auctions, decide on the
terms question** (§6 rows 2, 5, 6). The same tooling is unambiguous on sources
without such clauses, and once permission exists.

## 9. What paper trading can and can't prove

**Auction-selection performance (paper CAN measure):**

- What units clear at, by size
- Cancellation rate
- Whether our max bids would win (deal flow)
- Whether we win only where we're the most optimistic (winner's curse:
  estimate ÷ price on wins vs. all)
- How much time the research takes

**Real liquidation performance (paper CANNOT measure):**

- What was in the boxes
- What it sold for
- How long it took
- How much went to the dump
- The real hours

Every paper profit figure is ESTIMATED by the model under test.

### Evidence threshold before *considering* a first <$300 experiment

This is not a recommendation to buy. All of these must hold:

1. **Sample:** ≥ 8 weeks of capture and ≥ 100 closed 5x5/5x10 auctions with
   observed outcomes (sold price or cancelled), of which ≥ 40 had a FORWARD
   paper decision recorded before close.
2. **Price room:** median all-in acquisition (winning bid × 1.18) for small
   units sits below the **cash** break-even of a MEDIUM load at a pessimistic
   $500 resale (~$250 today). In other words, typical prices leave room even
   when contents disappoint.
3. **Deal flow:** `manual_v1` would have won ≥ 10% of the auctions it bid on
   (≥ 5 wins) under the *conservative* settlement price.
4. **No winner's-curse flag:** on simulated wins, our estimate ÷ winning price
   isn't in the top decile of that ratio across all auctions we estimated.
5. **Verified costs:** dump pricing confirmed by phone; your age known; a named
   friend with an SUV or pickup available on 48 h notice (or a U-Haul plan);
   dry, secure storage space identified.
6. **Measured selling time:** ≥ 10 of your own items sold with minutes logged,
   and the implied economic profit at *your* chosen $/h still positive in the
   grid for the typical observed clearing price.
7. **Pre-registration:** the experiment unit gets a recorded paper decision,
   photo-based estimate and max bid *before* bidding; spend cap $300 all-in;
   every item, hour, trip and receipt logged afterwards.

Even then, **one unit calibrates process and cost, not the edge**. Valuation
calibration needs roughly 5–10 real units. That's a later decision.

6. Annual tax note in `report paper` (SE tax ≈ 14% above $400).

## 12. What changed the picture

**Better than v1 said:**

- Household disposal is priced by weight with no minimum: ~$0–185 for typical
  small units, not $253+ per visit.
- With labor out of the cash figures, a small locker's **cash risk is small**:
  a $100-bid 5x5 breaks even in cash at ~$180–420 of resale.
- The same test auction's max bid rose from $150 to $281.
- SUV capacity is larger than guessed. Sales tax is 8.20%. Licensed purchases
  can be tax-exempt.
- Free outlets exist: arc Thrift furniture pickup, Goodwill drop-off, county
  e-waste, scrap metal.
- Public Storage and CubeSmart publish auction schedules: a legitimate
  discovery channel.
- Local facilities may be willing to share past sale results, which would be a
  legitimate backtest set.

**Worse than v1 said:**

- **Selling labor dominates** (~80% of hours). Paying yourself $25–40/h needs
  ~$50–100+ per sale. Lockers full of $10–25 household goods lose money in
  economic terms however cheaply they're bought.
- A regular reseller must collect sales tax on local sales (~5.7% of local
  resale) and pay self-employment tax (~14% of annual profit). v1 had neither.
- **The auction pool is small** (~12–58 scheduled per week countywide, fewer
  small units). Lockerfox's terms restrict manual monitoring too. The data
  plan hinges on StorageTreasures permission or facility cooperation.
- Auctions can be cancelled **even after you win**.
- If you're under 21, Home Depot's cheap van is out (U-Haul still works).
- A half-full 5x10 needs a rental. Borrowed cars only work for light loads.
- There's no published data on winning bids, cancellation rates or storage
  flipping profits, so there's nothing to calibrate against except our own
  forward capture.
- Marin still looks better only because its dump costs are guesses.
