# Research area: referral and campus ambassador engine

**Status:** research and conceptual design (2026-10-04). **Nothing is built.** No referral feature
ships before launch, before provider permission (approval questions 27–31) and before counsel
review. **Decision refs:** D-024, D-028, D-030, D-031.

## 1. Design principles (non-negotiable)

1. **Paid only for real, approved economic activity.** A referrer earns only when someone they
   referred generates a qualifying, **provider-approved** conversion that has cleared the reversal
   window. Signups, installs or clicks alone earn nothing.
2. **One level only.** A referrer earns from users they referred directly, never from users their
   referrals go on to refer. No reward for recruiting recruiters, no downstream commissions, no
   overrides, no tiers based on team size.
3. **No buy-in.** Participation is free. There is nothing to purchase to refer or to stay active.
4. **Bounded.** Per-referral rewards are fixed by a versioned policy. Each referred user can
   generate at most one referral reward (or a time-bounded set, if ever adopted). There are caps
   per referrer per period.
5. **Voluntary and individual.** People participate as individuals. No quotas, no mandatory
   participation, no pledging or initiation requirements, no group penalties. (Organization-level
   payouts: see §8.)
6. **Referral rewards are acquisition cost** and appear in contribution-margin calculations as CAC,
   measured as **Referral Contribution Margin** and compared directly with paid and organic cohorts
   (EXPERIMENTS "Channel cohort comparison").
7. **Performance-based compensation is allowed, and is the intended model (D-028).**
   Referrers and campus ambassadors may be paid per directly referred user who produces an
   eligible, provider-approved economic conversion. This is gated on provider permission (Q27–29,
   Q33) and legal review.

## 2. Economics

### Definitions (consistent with EXPERIMENTS.md)

The founders' illustration: provider revenue $50, user reward $38, referral reward $2, "remaining
gross spread $10". Under our metric definitions, **gross spread is $12** (NPR − user rewards). The
$2 referral reward is **acquisition cost**, so it sits below gross spread:

| Line (illustrative, not a policy)                         | Amount                |
| --------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------- |
| Net publisher revenue (after reversals)                   | $50.00                |
| − User rewards                                            | $38.00                |
| **= Gross spread**                                        | **$12.00**            |
| − Referral reward (CAC)                                   | $2.00                 |
| − Payment fees, fraud loss, support, working-capital cost | measured, not assumed |
| **= Contribution profit**                                 | ≤ $10.00              |

This matters because comparing channels needs CAC kept separate from spread: a $2 referral reward
competes with $X of paid social, not with the user's reward.

### Affordability

Amounts are policy parameters (`referral_policy`), never constants in code. The ceiling should come
from measured data:

```
Let, per referred signup over a horizon H (e.g. 90 days):
  GS  = E[gross spread generated by a referred user], measured NET of provider reversals
        (for the REFERRED cohort, not assumed equal to organic)
  V   = E[variable costs: payment fees, fraud loss, support, working-capital cost]
  q   = P(the referral reward is actually PAID), i.e. qualifies and survives the hold
  R   = referral reward,  B = optional welcome bonus to the referred user (paid with prob. q_B)
  r   = expected amount recovered on rewards paid but later reversed (usually ≈ 0; count it only
        if a recovery policy exists and is measured)

Expected acquisition cost per referred signup = q·R + q_B·B − r
Contribution per referred signup              = GS − V − (q·R + q_B·B − r)
Policy constraint:  q·R + q_B·B − r ≤ GS − V − target_contribution
```

Note on reversals: a referral reward paid on a conversion that is later reversed is already counted
in q·R (the money was spent), and the lost revenue is already reflected in GS (measured net of
reversals). Adding a separate reversal term would double-count. The hold period exists to make q
smaller in the bad cases, not to change the formula.

The referred cohort's GS, q and ρ must be **measured separately**. Referred traffic may be more
loyal, or it may be more fraudulent. Do not assume it equals organic traffic.

### Policy shape (recommended)

- A fixed amount per qualified referral, plus a cap per referrer per month. Optionally, the reward
  is tied to a minimum cumulative approved NPR from the referred user within N days. That is
  harder to game than "any first conversion", because tiny conversions cannot trigger it.
- The policy version is **frozen at link time** (like the route lock), so later changes don't
  retroactively alter what a referrer was promised.
- No percentage-of-lifetime commissions in v1. If ever adopted, they must be time-bounded and
  single-level.

## 3. Lifecycle (conceptual)

```
LINKED ──(referred user generates provider-APPROVED qualifying conversion)──▶ QUALIFIED
QUALIFIED ──(longest relevant reversal window + risk review pass)──▶ AVAILABLE ──▶ PAID
LINKED ──(no qualifying event within window)──▶ EXPIRED
any pre-PAID ──(self-referral / duplicate account / fraud / policy breach)──▶ VOIDED (reason, appealable)
QUALIFIED/AVAILABLE ──(qualifying conversion reversed by provider)──▶ REVERSED
PAID ──(qualifying conversion reversed later)──▶ REVERSED_AFTER_PAYOUT → receivable or offset (policy + terms)
```

Referral rewards use the same double-entry ledger as user rewards (account
`acquisition_expense:referral`). Provider reversals of the underlying conversion **always**
propagate to the referral reward.

## 4. Attribution

- Each user may own one referral code. Ambassador codes are a distinct kind with their own policy.
- **Single attribution, captured at signup.** A code is taken from the `?ref=` link on the signup
  page or entered manually. It is immutable after signup. Support corrections are allowed only with
  an audit-log entry.
- **No tracking cookies** (consistent with D-011). If a visitor leaves and returns without the link,
  the code is lost unless entered manually. This is accepted for privacy.
- Referral attribution is to _our_ platform. It never alters provider attribution or route locks.
  Ambassadors link to **our site only, never to provider tracking links**. Sending provider links
  directly could make them unapproved sub-publishers (at least one candidate network's terms
  prohibit "unapproved sub-publishers" and "unapproved sources"; source: Adparagon offerwall terms,
  retrieved 2026-10-04).

## 5. Fraud controls

| Threat                      | Controls                                                                                                                                                                                 |
| --------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Self-referral               | Block when referrer and referred share verified identity, payout account, device identifier, or IP + user-agent in a short window; email normalization (dots, `+tags`) for matching only |
| Duplicate / farmed accounts | Verification before first withdrawal; one payout account per person; device and risk signals (with privacy-policy disclosure)                                                            |
| Referral rings              | Graph checks: clusters of mutual referrals; referred users whose only activity is the minimum qualifying action; synchronized activity                                                   |
| Low-quality qualifying      | Qualifying threshold on approved NPR, not on "any conversion"; hold until reversal windows clear                                                                                         |
| Velocity abuse              | Per-referrer caps and rate limits; manual review above thresholds                                                                                                                        |
| Provider-flagged fraud      | Any provider rejection or reversal for the referred user voids or reverses the referral reward                                                                                           |

Risk signals lead to **holds and human review**, never automatic accusations. There is a documented
appeal path. A referrer sees only aggregate counts and statuses for their referrals, never referred
users' identities or activity.

## 6. Provider permission gate

Before enabling referral or ambassador traffic for **any** provider's offers:

- Q27: Are referral-program users (incentivized to sign up by a referral bonus) permitted traffic?
- Q28: Are campus or ambassador programs permitted? Are they considered sub-publishers?
- Q29: Are referral bonuses funded from your payouts acceptable, or are any restrictions placed on
  how payouts may be used?

**Architecture consequence:** each user carries an `acquisition_channel` (organic, referral,
ambassador, paid_social, …). Each provider (and, where needed, each campaign) carries
`allowed_traffic_sources`. Offer eligibility filters on both. A provider that forbids referral
traffic simply never shows its offers to referral-acquired users. Routing must respect this too.

## 7. Compliance considerations (questions for counsel)

- **FTC Endorsement Guides** (CONFIRMED, ftc.gov): ambassadors and referrers have a _material
  connection_, including "the possibility of being paid". They must disclose it clearly and with the
  endorsement itself. A bare "ambassador" is not enough; "{Brand} Ambassador" or "ad" is. We are
  responsible for training and monitoring. Ambassadors may not make earnings claims we cannot
  substantiate (the copy guard rules apply to the ambassador kit).
- **Pyramid / MLM risk:** the hallmark of a pyramid is compensation driven by recruitment rather
  than by real economic activity. Single-level design, no buy-in and conversion-qualified rewards
  are intended to keep well clear of it. Needs counsel confirmation.
- **Tax:** referral rewards and ambassador stipends are income (1099 obligations and thresholds to
  confirm). Ambassadors paid stipends need contractor agreements and a worker-classification review.
- **Contests:** no prizes for "top recruiters". That avoids contest and sweepstakes rules and
  reduces pressure.
- **Messaging:** no tools that send automated texts or upload contacts (TCPA and CAN-SPAM exposure).
  Sharing is the referrer's own link in their own words.
- **Age:** referrers and referred users must be 18+. Some college freshmen are 17.

## 8. Campus ambassador program

### Why it could work (hypotheses, not facts)

- Students are price-sensitive and mobile-heavy, and many use Android (unverified; E008 measures the
  platform split).
- Trust travels through friend groups; a peer explaining "here is exactly what it pays and what it
  takes" fits our transparency positioning better than ads do.
- Possibly much lower CAC than paid social (to be measured against the E004 baseline).

### Guardrails specific to campus

- **Compensation model:** an ambassador earns per **directly referred** user who produces an
  eligible, provider-approved conversion that clears the reversal window, under the same policy,
  holds and fraud controls as any referrer (§2–5), possibly with an ambassador-specific policy
  version. Nothing for signups, installs or clicks, nothing for recruiting other ambassadors, and no
  downstream commissions.
- **Individuals, not organizations.** Ambassadors act personally, not on behalf of fraternities,
  sororities, clubs, teams or dorms. We recommend **keeping** the rule that organizations are not
  paid per member conversion. An organization's leadership has authority over members, so per-member
  payments to the organization create exactly the pressure the guardrails forbid. This was retained
  as an anti-coercion guardrail. If the founders want organization-level performance compensation,
  it needs an explicit decision and legal review.
- **Code of conduct:** no pressure, no participation requirements, no tying to membership, rank,
  pledging or initiation, no hazing contexts, no messaging people who have not opted in, no use of
  university names or marks, no posting where campus rules prohibit it, and disclosure every time.
  Breach ends the relationship and voids pending rewards.
- **Campus rules:** universities commonly restrict commercial solicitation in residence halls and
  the use of their marks (INFERENCE; verify per campus before any activity there).
- **Student-athletes:** NIL compensation rules and school/state disclosure requirements apply.
  Recommend excluding student-athlete status from pilot recruiting to avoid that complexity, or
  requiring their own compliance confirmation.
- **No leaderboards or competitions** between people or groups.

### Testing before building infrastructure

Pre-launch we can test **distribution reach and audience fit** with what already exists: the
waitlist captures `?ref=` source tags (`[a-z0-9_-]{1,64}`), and `npm run waitlist:report` aggregates
signups by source and platform with no personal data. See EXPERIMENTS E008 (pre-launch) and E009
(post-launch referral pilot).

Before launch there are no conversions, so performance pay cannot apply yet. Pre-launch
ambassadors must **not** be paid per waitlist signup: that pays for non-economic activity and
invites junk signups. Options for E008: unpaid founding ambassadors with clear terms (told that
conversion-based compensation is planned, subject to provider permission and legal review), or a
flat stipend for time. Both are disclosed as material connections. Performance compensation starts
in E009, after launch, provider permission and legal review.
